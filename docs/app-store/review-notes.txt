Purpose and audience
Deleks is a single-player, turn-based puzzle game for anyone who likes short logic games, from older children to adults. Robots move one square toward you each turn; you survive by stepping, teleporting or pulsing so that robots collide with each other and with the scrap heaps they leave. A level takes a minute or two. The app needs no account or connection and has no ads or tracking.

How to use it
No setup or login is needed. Launch the app and a game starts.
- Tap anywhere on the board and your piece steps one square toward that spot, diagonals included; tap your own piece to wait a turn. On a hardware keyboard, the keys 1-9 move as on a numeric keypad (5 waits), and T, P and L work the Teleport, Pulse and Last stand buttons.
- Teleport (below the board) jumps to a random empty square.
- Pulse (shown as "Pulse ×" and the number left) scraps every robot next to you. You start with one, gain one each level, and unused pulses carry over.
- Last stand keeps you in place, turn after turn, until the level is cleared or you are caught. Robots scrapped during it score double points.
- Clearing every robot shows "Level N cleared" with a "Next level" button. Being caught shows "Caught!" with a "New game" button.
- The "Safe-move hints" and "Modern look" switches are below the board. "About…" in the title bar shows the version, the license, a link to the source code and a short history of the genre.

External services
None. The app makes no network connections and uses no third-party SDKs, analytics, authentication, payment or AI services; all of its code runs on the device. The only outside links are two in the About box (origamisoftware.com and the GitHub source page), which open in Safari.

Regional differences
None. The app works the same in every region.
